Milestones

A history of progress

1968

Intel Founded

Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore rename NM Electronics to Intel Corporation, laying the foundation for decades of technological innovation.

1971

First Microprocessor

Intel debuts the 4004, the world's first commercial microprocessor, igniting the microprocessor revolution.

1978

8086 Processor

The 8086 establishes the x86 architecture that drives countless PCs and servers in the modern era.

1985

386 Processor

Intel introduces the 386 processor with 32-bit architecture, ushering in a new era of performance and multitasking.

2006

Peak GHG Emissions

Intel's highest annual operational greenhouse gas emissions prompt major investment in abatement, renewable energy, and efficient manufacturing.

2020

RISE Strategy

Intel launches its Responsible, Inclusive, Sustainable, Enabling strategy and 2030 goals for climate, water, and waste.

2022

Net-Zero By 2040

Intel commits to net-zero greenhouse gas emissions across global operations by 2040.

2023

Renewable Electricity

Intel reaches 99% renewable electricity usage worldwide, lowering carbon emissions and advancing its long-term goals.

2024

Sustainability Summit

Intel brings suppliers, government officials, and industry leaders together to shape sustainable semiconductor manufacturing.

Our approach

Sustainability priorities

RISE Strategy

As part of its RISE (Responsible, Inclusive, Sustainable, and Enabled) strategy, Intel has set ambitious goals for 2030, including driving industry-wide progress in climate action, water management, and waste reduction.

Learn more

Commitment

In 2022, Intel committed to achieving net-zero greenhouse gas emissions (Scopes 1 and 2) by 2040. This commitment is based on decades of environmental initiatives and partnerships throughout the semiconductor industry.

Learn more

Water and Waste

Intel conserves billions of gallons of water annually and cooperates with local communities to restore watersheds. Intel also recycles materials to reduce waste and promote a circular economy.

Insights

How Intel is building a sustainable future

Intel continues to reduce its footprint by investing in lower-carbon manufacturing, renewable electricity, and efficiency gains across its global operations.

Water conservation, watershed recovery, and materials recycling help Intel move toward more resilient, efficient production systems with less waste.

The company’s roadmap combines technology advancement with broader inclusion, workforce development, and partnerships that support communities worldwide.
